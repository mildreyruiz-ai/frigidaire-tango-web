Since 1977

From the Outkids to Modern Vintage Wave

In July 1977 Carlo Casale came back from London and started a punk band, the Outkids, with guitarist Stefano Dal Col. The band became Trash, and once Marco Breda arrived on keyboards they became Frigidaire Tango. The name mixes the cult comics magazine Frigidaire with a word Casale added on impulse.

They sang in English (Charlie Out, Stefano Dal Col, Marco Breda, J.M. Le Baptiste). They mixed prog, krautrock and glam with punk energy and a cold synth edge. Their 1981 debut The Cock came out when they were all under 20. In 2010 the French magazine Tsugi named it one of the 100 essential lesser-known rock LPs.

A day in the studio cost 500,000 lire, a month's wages.

They toured Italy with The Sound. RAI 1 filmed them for the 1984 documentary L'ultimo concerto, and they played the Barcelona festival alongside Litfiba, Diaframma and CCCP. Then, after about thirty concerts, they played their last show on Treviso's city walls in July 1986.

Twenty years later the Freezer Box brought back the old material, and the trio started playing again. L'illusione del volo (2009), produced by Giorgio Canali, was their first album in Italian. A 20-artist tribute followed in 2013, and Modern Vintage Wave in 2026.

The full story

Chapter by chapter

1977–1980 · From Outkids to Trash

Frigidaire Tango began in 1977, when Carlo Casale and Stefano Dal Col started working together under the spell of British punk and formed the Outkids. The band became Trash and, once Marco Breda joined on keyboards, its sound widened: progressive rock, krautrock and glam met punk energy, and the group settled into Italy's new wave scene.

1981–1983 · The Cock and Russian Dolls

In 1981, now called Frigidaire Tango, they recorded their first LP, The Cock, for Young Records. Two years later, with Franco Turesso as a second keyboardist, they produced the mini-LP Russian Dolls themselves. Two promotional videos were made, they toured Italy with The Sound, and Radio Rai played the single "Recall".

1984–1986 · RAI, Barcelona and the end

RAI 1 filmed the band for L'ultimo concerto, a documentary by Piergiorgio Gay about the tension between playing music and earning a living. Their songs appeared on the Rockgarage and Body Section compilations, they played the Barcelona biennale, and they contributed "The Romantic Lifeguard" to the P.E.A.C.E. benefit compilation for Amnesty International. In the summer of 1986 the band split: the members found it hard to reconcile music with their careers, and they no longer agreed on where the group was heading.

1988–2008 · The Vindicators, the Freezer Box and the return

Most of the line-up regrouped as the Vindicators (1988–91), a beat, rock'n'roll and R&B project that recorded two LPs. The band's own story came back in 2006–07 with The Freezer Box (Alma Records): three discs and a 50-page book, with The Cock plus two outtakes, Music for Us, the second album recorded in 1983–85 and never released until then, and a previously unreleased concert from Barcelona with six extra live tracks. The old songs brought the band back together, and in 2008 they returned to the studio.

2009–2013 · L'illusione del volo and the tribute

L'illusione del volo came out in October 2009 on La Tempesta Dischi. The band produced it and Giorgio Canali mixed it, with guests such as Federico Fiumani, Fabio Trentini, Aldo Tagliapietra, Diego Galeri and Miss Xox. Videos followed for "Soffia" (directed by Stefano Poletti) and "Natural mente" (Jacopo Rondinelli and Peppo Bianchessi). In September 2010 the French magazine Tsugi named The Cock among the 100 essential lesser-known rock LPs, and in 2013 Go Down Records released a tribute with covers by Giorgio Canali, Sick Tamburo, Ilenia Volpe, Federico Fiumani and others.

2014–2026 · Rediscovery and Modern Vintage Wave

The band's place in the Italian eighties was recognised again. Livia Satriano's book Gli altri Ottanta (Agenzia X, 2014) covers the post-punk scene they belong to, and in 2023 Rumore 100 listed Russian Dolls among the 100 essential Italian new wave and post-punk records. In 2026 came new music: the singles "Fears" (January) and "Non sei raggiungibile" (March), then Modern Vintage Wave on Psicolabel, ten songs picked from about thirty unreleased recordings made between 2008 and 2025.

Timeline

1977 to today

  1. Jul 1977

    Carlo Casale and Stefano Dal Col form the punk band Outkids, after Casale comes back from a trip to London.

  2. 1979–80

    Renamed Trash, with Maurizio Battistella (drums) and Diego Negrello (bass). The 1980 demo tape for The Cock is recorded.

  3. 1980–81

    Marco Breda joins on keyboards and the band becomes Frigidaire Tango.

  4. 1981

    Debut LP The Cock (Young Records, YN/B 6006). Some sources date it 1982.

  5. 1983

    Franco Turesso joins as second keyboardist. Self-produced mini-LP Russian Dolls ("Recall", "Vanity Fair") with promo videos made by local TV. Italian tour with The Sound. RAI radio and TV slots on Orecchiocchio and Diskogenia.

  6. 1983–84

    Tracks on the Rockgarage Vol. 2 and Vol. 4 compilations. "Vanity Fair" appears on Rockerilla's Body Section.

  7. 1984

    RAI 1 documentary L'ultimo concerto, directed by Piergiorgio Gay.

  8. 1985

    Play the Biennale / Tendencias festival in Barcelona alongside Diaframma, Litfiba, CCCP and Avion Travel.

  9. 1986

    "The Romantic Lifeguard" on the Amnesty International P.E.A.C.E. compilation. Final concert on Treviso's city walls, July 1986.

  10. 1988–91

    Members run The Vindicators, a 1950s–60s rock'n'roll and R&B project that records two LPs.

  11. 2006–07

    The Freezer Box (Alma Records): 3 discs and a 50-page booklet, including the unreleased LP Music for Us and Live in Barcelona '85.

  12. 2008

    Reunion recordings with producer Giorgio Canali. Back on stage with Ran at La Gabbia, San Giorgio in Bosco (12 Jan).

  13. 2009

    L'illusione del volo (La Tempesta Dischi, October), their first album in Italian. Produced by the band and mixed by Giorgio Canali, with guests including Federico Fiumani (Diaframma), Fabio Trentini, Aldo Tagliapietra (Le Orme), Diego Galeri and Miss Xox. About 15 live dates followed in 2010.

  14. 2009–10

    Videos for "Soffia" (2009, directed by Stefano Poletti) and "Natural mente" (2010, directed by Jacopo Rondinelli and Peppo Bianchessi), both from L'illusione del volo.

  15. Sep 2010

    French magazine Tsugi names The Cock one of the "100 essential lesser-known rock LPs".

  16. 2013

    Tribute album on Go Down Records, with covers by Giorgio Canali, Sick Tamburo, Ilenia Volpe, Federico Fiumani and others. Synthetic Shadows reissues the 1980 demo tape on vinyl. Tribute concert at Maximum Festival, Zero Branco (TV).

  17. 2014

    Livia Satriano's book Gli altri Ottanta (Agenzia X), on the Italian post-punk scene, includes the band.

  18. Aug–Sep 2023

    Rumore 100 (issue 3, edited by Luca Frazzi) lists Russian Dolls among the 100 essential Italian new wave and post-punk records.

  19. Jan 2026

    Single "Fears". "Non sei raggiungibile" follows in March.

  20. Apr–May 2026

    Modern Vintage Wave (Psicolabel): 10 songs picked from about 30 unreleased recordings made between 2008 and 2025.

Quick answers

The band in brief

Who are Frigidaire Tango?

Frigidaire Tango are an Italian new wave band from Bassano del Grappa. Carlo Casale and Stefano Dal Col started it in 1977 as the Outkids, and it became Frigidaire Tango once keyboardist Marco Breda joined.

What was Frigidaire Tango's first album?

Their first LP was The Cock, released in 1981 on Young Records. In 2010 the French magazine Tsugi named it one of the 100 essential lesser-known rock LPs.

When did Frigidaire Tango split and reunite?

The band played its last show in July 1986 on Treviso's city walls. The 2006–07 box set The Freezer Box (Alma Records) brought the old material back, and the band started playing again. L'illusione del volo (2009) was their first album in Italian.

What is Frigidaire Tango's latest album?

Modern Vintage Wave, released in 2026 on Psicolabel.
